## Black-hole shadows / Kerr spin / EHT  *(directly validates the renderer)*

- **Bayesian Inference of Primary Hair from Black Hole Shadows in Generalized
  Proca Theory** — J.-Z. She and S.-M. Wu, 2026, *arXiv preprint* (gr-qc). Takes
  an exact rotating solution of generalized Proca theory; a chosen coupling
  slice gives a one-parameter, noncircular Kerr deformation that depends on spin
  and latitude and vanishes in the nonrotating limit while the vector field stays
  nontrivial. Numerical ray tracing shows a modest shadow enlargement and a
  changed lensing pattern. Bayesian inference on the shadow area-equivalent
  radius and axis ratio from EHT M87* and Sgr A* gives joint hair–spin–inclination
  constraints: the M87* posterior favors relatively low spin with a pronounced
  spin–hair anticorrelation; Sgr A* gives upper limits on spin and hair with
  inclination essentially unconstrained. Constraint on one specific deformation
  family, not a model-independent measurement. *tier: preprint /
  observational_constraint.* https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24996
- **Prospects for submillimeter VLBI of black hole photon rings with a
  stratospheric balloon-borne telescope** — M. Bagchi et al., 2026, *arXiv
  preprint* (astro-ph.IM; submitted to ApJ per arXiv comment). Forecast, using
  2012–2022 weather, for a 2.5 m telescope at 35–40 km (successor to the 22 GHz
  BVEX pathfinder) added to ground VLBI at 345–690 GHz: the balloon adds little
  baseline length but reaches higher frequencies. On an ALMA–balloon baseline,
  Sgr A* at 460 GHz sits at the detection threshold, with ten or more 20 s
  detections on about half of simulated April days, lying beyond the polarimetric
  photon-ring transition where the n=1 ring dominates the polarized visibility
  amplitudes; M87* at 460 GHz needs frequency phase transfer from a 230 GHz
  reference. In Fisher forecasts a 460 GHz ring-geometry constraint requires
  balloons but is weaker than at 345 GHz, where balloons add little. Proposed
  instrument, not data; thematic neighbor of Thiel et al. (2610.19204) in
  2026-10-16. *tier: preprint / numerical_approximation.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24396
- **The accretion flow and jet of M87: broadband modelling across spatial
  scales** — E. M. Gutiérrez et al., 2026, *arXiv preprint* (astro-ph.HE;
  astro-ph.GA cross-list; submitted to a journal per arXiv comment). Fits the EHT
  collaboration's multi-wavelength M87 campaign with a radiatively inefficient
  accretion flow (RIAF) with winds plus a new multi-zone, initially
  magnetically dominated jet that solves the transport equation for non-thermal
  electrons injected, advected and cooled along the jet, comparing each radio flux
  with jet emission inside an aperture set by the observation's angular scale.
  The comparison favours the RIAF, not the jet, producing the compact 230 GHz
  emission, with the jet dominating lower-frequency radio, optical/UV and X-ray
  but unable to produce the observed γ-rays; the horizon accretion rate comes out
  at 1.1×10⁻⁵ of Eddington (consistent with EHT polarimetry) and each jet carries
  about a quarter of the accretion power, 1.2×10⁴³ erg s⁻¹. Model-fitting study,
  not a model-independent measurement; companion in topic to Pesce et al.
  (2610.20961) in 2026-10-19. *tier: preprint / numerical_approximation.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26434

## Numerical relativity / ringdown / QNM  *(adjacent observational GR)*

- **Moving Beyond Quasinormal Modes and Reinterpreting Direct Waves in Binary
  Black Hole Merger-Ringdowns** — R. Dyer et al., 2026, *arXiv preprint* (gr-qc;
  astro-ph.HE cross-list). Presents three independent strands of evidence against
  the suggestion, drawn from perturbation theory and an effective-one-body
  picture, that a measurable "direct wave" in the ringdown originates from well
  inside the remnant's light ring or near its horizon. Confirms an early-time part
  of the ringdown that quasinormal modes cannot model directly, but finds no
  evidence it is a direct wave as previously defined, and ties its properties to
  the remnant's light ring rather than its horizon. Counterpoint to earlier
  direct-wave entries (e.g. 2026-06-26, 2026-07-08, 2026-10-15, notably the
  horizon-reflectivity null test of 2610.12429, which uses the direct wave as its
  normalization). *tier: preprint / numerical_approximation.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25176
- **The shape of nonlinearity in binary-black-hole dynamics** — T. Islam, 2026,
  *arXiv preprint* (gr-qc). Builds a modular time-domain Teukolsky framework that
  isolates finite-mass-ratio corrections to the orbital dynamics while keeping the
  radiation at first perturbative order, and compares with numerical relativity.
  For nonspinning binaries the available second-order dissipative flux removes
  87% of the inspiral-rate discrepancy, bringing it to about 1% of NR; for
  precessing binaries a ν² sin²ι dependence appears in the inspiral time
  rescaling that leading orbit-averaged post-Newtonian terms do not explain; near
  merger the same correction removes only 1–2%, and the merger offset is instead
  accounted for by the difference between the initial Kerr background and the
  remnant, with 96% of the leading predicted frequency shift coming from the
  change in spin rather than mass. Releases 1571 time-domain waveforms for 348
  binaries (the Vishveshwara Catalog). *tier: preprint / numerical_approximation.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27164
- **Nonlinear Response of Schwarzschild Black Holes at Arbitrary Perturbative
  Order** — J. Chen and Y.-C. Xie, 2026, *arXiv preprint* (gr-qc; hep-ph, hep-th
  cross-lists). Derives recursive integration-by-parts identities that reduce the
  inhomogeneous solutions of the Regge–Wheeler–Zerilli equation to a small set of
  master integrals, giving nonlinear response kernels of Schwarzschild black holes
  at arbitrary perturbative order, plus a closed ODE system for fast, stable
  numerical evaluation of those integrals. Demonstrated by computing quadratic,
  cubic and quartic quasinormal-mode coupling coefficients; Schwarzschild only, 7
  pages. *tier: preprint / numerical_approximation.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27048
- **How accurate is Spectral Einstein Code (SpEC)? And what limits its
  accuracy?** — H. Chaudhary et al., 2026, *arXiv preprint* (gr-qc). Studies error
  sources in SpEC binary-black-hole simulations, concentrating on the inspiral
  because it runs longest and carries most of the waveform error. Targeted changes
  to simulation parameters and to resolution in different parts of the
  computational domain reduce constraint violations and waveform differences; at
  higher resolution the more fundamental limits are floating-point error and large
  differences in the scales of the evolved variables, which the authors say will
  need substantial changes to both the code and its evolution formulation, and
  they expect many of the findings to carry over to other NR codes. *tier: preprint
  / numerical_approximation.* https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24283
- **Evidence for orbital eccentricity supports a hierarchical origin for
  GW231123** — B. Wang et al., 2026, *arXiv preprint* (astro-ph.HE; gr-qc
  cross-list). Re-analyses GW231123 (the most massive BBH merger observed to date)
  over 11–448 Hz with the eccentric, spin-precessing TEOBResumS–Dalí effective-one-body
  model, including detector calibration uncertainty. Reports e = 0.34 (+0.12/−0.23)
  at 5.5 Hz (90% credibility) and a Bayes factor of 10.2 for the eccentric over
  the quasi-circular model; injection–recovery runs suggest that analyses starting
  at 20 Hz can hide the eccentricity. Including eccentricity weakens the
  preference for a near-extremal secondary spin, lowering its posterior median to
  about 0.7, which the authors read as a hierarchical merger in a dense
  environment. Result depends on the waveform model; same event as Bini et al.
  in 2026-07-13, who found its headline masses and spins robust to waveform
  systematics. *tier: preprint /
  observational_constraint.* https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12205

## Quantum gravity / information paradox  *(speculative context — label, do not let it touch the kernel)*

- **Extremal black hole mimickers: geometry, stability, and long-delay signal
  trains** — S. N. Solodukhin, 2026, *arXiv preprint* (hep-th; gr-qc, math-ph
  cross-lists; v2 corrects typos). Constructs a horizonless mimicker of an
  extremal black hole with a single asymptotically flat region and an infinitely
  long throat approaching M₁,₁ × S². Aretakis-like modes arise in a local analysis
  of the throat but do not produce a physical instability; a scalar perturbation
  initially in the throat yields, at late times, a train of signals separated by
  t_delay ~ 1/λ (λ ≪ 1 the deformation parameter), parametrically longer than the
  echo time t_echo ~ ln(1/λ) of non-extremal mimickers, with the periodicity tied
  to periodic geodesics on S². An extremal-Kerr extension is expected to give a
  quasi-periodic train, and the paper comments on implications for unitarity in
  AdS/CFT. Hypothetical geometry, no data. *tier: speculative_extension.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11664
- **Black hole free energy landscapes from constrained instantons** — S.-P. Wu et
  al., 2026, *arXiv preprint* (gr-qc; hep-th cross-list). Uses gravitational
  path-integral factorization to give a semiclassical reading of generalized black
  hole free-energy landscapes at fixed temperature and angular velocities:
  constrained saddles labelled by partial-Neumann data on an auxiliary
  hypersurface have action I = β(E − ΩJ) − S, and interior stationary points of the
  landscape correspond to smooth equilibrium saddles when the retained variations
  independently span the entropy and angular-momentum directions. Extended to
  local parity-even f(Riemann) theories without curvature derivatives, with Wald
  entropy; checked on Schwarzschild, Kerr, five-dimensional static AdS black holes
  in Einstein–Gauss–Bonnet gravity and the rotating BTZ branch of new massive
  gravity. Formal semiclassical construction, 59 pages. *tier:
  speculative_extension.* https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26094
- **BPS black holes in flat space from quantum mechanics** — X. Shi and G. J.
  Turiaci, 2026, *arXiv preprint* (hep-th). Derives an exact formula counting BPS
  configurations of the D-brane system corresponding to black holes in N=8
  supergravity from type II strings on T⁶, relating the BPS equations to a
  solvable modified Calogero–Moser system. The count agrees with the prediction
  from the modified elliptic genus of the D1-D5 system, a nontrivial test of the
  string dualities connecting the two descriptions; the authors also propose
  numerical tests of this quantum-mechanical model, which they call simpler than
  BFSS as a model of quantum black holes. Supersymmetric string-theory setting,
  not Schwarzschild or Kerr. *tier: speculative_extension.*
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18951
- **Black Holes are about Quantum Information** — A. Corbeel and S. De Haro, 2026,
  *arXiv preprint* (physics.hist-ph; hep-th, quant-ph cross-lists). Reviews and
  analyses quantum-information approaches to black hole evaporation, focusing on
  the Page-time paradox and the Hayden–Preskill model, in which the black hole is a
  finite-dimensional quantum system whose dynamics is a quantum circuit. Picks out
  fast scrambling and entanglement as a resource as the two features behind the
  information release, and argues that this marks a broader shift toward quantum
  information theory as an operational modelling framework that avoids earlier
  philosophical objections because it does not rest on epistemic ignorance.
  Philosophy-of-physics review that assumes unitary evaporation. *tier:
  speculative_extension / review.* https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28185
